  - question: What is the oil yield of the HN426 hydraulic oil press?
    answer: The oil yield depends on the raw material. For example, sesame yields 42-54%, peanuts 40-50%, and rapeseed 35-45%. The HN426's 500-ton pressing force and bar-type slotted barrel design achieve approximately 2-3% higher yield (based on internal testing, results vary) than traditional drilled barrel presses.
  - question: What raw materials can the HN426 press?
    answer: The HN426 can press sesame, peanut, rapeseed, sunflower seed, flaxseed, tea seed, soybean, cottonseed, and more. It is especially designed for cold pressing hard-shell materials like walnut kernels, in-shell walnuts, and apricot kernels.
  - question: How long does one pressing cycle take?
    answer: Cold pressing requires longer cycle times to preserve oil quality at low temperatures. A typical cold pressing cycle takes approximately 2 hours per batch depending on the material and desired cake dryness. Hot pressing (with pre-roasting) completes in 30-40 minutes. The actual cycle time depends on factors like seed moisture content, desired cake dryness, and pressing temperature. The microcomputer control system automates the entire process from feeding to cake discharge.
  - question: What is the recommended raw material moisture content for cold pressing?
    answer: For cold pressing, the ideal moisture content range is 7-9%. This moisture level ensures smooth oil extraction while maintaining low-temperature conditions. Proper moisture control is critical — too high causes oil emulsification and yield loss, while too low increases friction and reduces throughput.
  - question: What pre-processing equipment is needed for cold pressing?
    answer: Cold pressing requires careful pre-processing steps including seed cleaning and color sorting to remove impurities, dehulling for high-shell-content materials with shell removal rate at least 95 percent, moisture conditioning to reach optimal 7-9 percent moisture, and gentle crushing to break seeds without generating heat below 60 degrees Celsius. Unlike hot pressing, cold pressing does not involve roasting, preserving natural nutrients and flavors.
  - question: What is the difference between single and double barrel configurations?
    answer: A single barrel press processes one batch at a time. A double barrel press has two barrels on sliding rails — while one barrel is pressing, you can load the other, nearly doubling your throughput. We recommend the double barrel for commercial and industrial operations.

## Overview

The HN426 Cold Press Hydraulic Oil Press is our dedicated cold pressing specialist, engineered to extract oil from delicate and hard-shell materials without heat degradation. With a 500-ton pressing force and 42 Mpa/cm² cake surface pressure, it delivers the power needed to cold press walnut kernels, in-shell walnuts, apricot kernels, yellowhorn, tiger nuts, and similar challenging materials — all fully extracted in a single cycle.

Built on a 3 cm thick steel plate frame, the HN426 combines structural robustness with precision hydraulic control. Cold pressing preserves the natural flavor, aroma, and nutritional value of your oil, commanding premium prices in the market. If you are producing high-value cold-pressed oils, this machine is designed to maximize both your oil yield and your profit margin.
