## Introduction

A hydraulic oil press is the heart of an oil mill, but the machine alone does not determine output or quality. What buyers actually pay for is a **production line**: cleaning, dehulling, conditioning, roasting, crushing, pressing, filtering, storing, and cake handling, each sized so that no station waits on the next. A balanced line with one 100 kg press can outproduce a larger press starved by a slow roaster or a clogged filter.

This guide walks through every station in a small-to-medium edible oil line, explains how to balance capacities, gives realistic space and utility figures, and ends with budget planning so you can talk to suppliers with numbers rather than guesses.

## Production Line Components

### 1. Seed Cleaning System

Oilseed arriving from the farm contains stones, soil, stalks, metal fragments, and empty husks. Every kilogram of impurity that reaches the press wastes pressure, abrades the barrel, and darkens the oil. A basic cleaning train includes:

- **Vibrating screen cleaner** — removes oversized and undersized particles on two or three sieves
- **Magnetic separator** — catches nails and metal dust before they enter the crusher
- **Gravity separator or destoner** — lifts out stones of the same size as the seed
- **Aspiration channel** — sucks off dust, husks, and light empty kernels

The cleaner should be rated at **1.5–2 times the pressing input rate**, because cleaning runs in short bursts while the press runs continuously.

### 2. Dehulling System

Hulls absorb oil and add wax to the crude oil, so removing them raises effective yield and reduces barrel wear.

- Disc or blade dehuller for sunflower and oats; hammer-type dehuller for peanut
- Peanut: aim for 95%+ hull removal; sesame: only dehull for light-coloured oil products
- Coconut requires a separate shelling and paring step before grating

Small cold-press operations selling rustic, full-flavoured oil sometimes skip dehulling — accept the trade-off consciously.

### 3. Moisture Conditioning System

Moisture is the single most influential variable in extraction. Seed that is too dry passes pressure without releasing oil; seed that is too wet produces sludge and emulsified oil.

- Steam conditioner (jacketed screw) for large lines; hot-air dryer or sun drying for small mills
- Hot pressing target: **5–8% moisture**; cold pressing target: **7–9%**
- Verify with a portable moisture meter every load, not by eye

### 4. Roasting System (Hot Pressing Only)

Roasting ruptures oil cells, develops the nutty aroma buyers associate with traditional oil, and lowers moisture before pressing.

- Drum roaster for even, continuous roasting; flat-bed pan for small-batch and sesame work
- Sesame: 160–180°C for 25–35 minutes; peanut: roughly 130°C (120–150°C range) for 20–30 minutes; rapeseed: 150–170°C for 20–25 minutes
- Output moisture after roasting: about 3–5%

Cold-press lines skip this station entirely — heat is what cold-press buyers are paying to avoid.

### 5. Seed Crushing System

Whole seeds press poorly because oil must travel too far to escape. Crushing to a coarse grit opens the cells.

- Roller crusher for soft seeds (peanut, sesame); hammer mill for copra and hard cakes
- Target particle size: **2–4 mm** for most seeds, 3–5 mm for hard nuts
- Over-crushing produces flour that blinds the barrel slots — coarser is usually better than finer

### 6. Hydraulic Oil Press

The press applies static high pressure to the grit inside a slotted barrel; oil drains through the slots while the solids form a cake.

- Current Haonuo line: [HN325](/products/standard-hydraulic-oil-press/) (325 t, ~27 MPa cake pressure), HN365 (365 t, 30 MPa), [HN426](/products/cold-press-hydraulic-oil-press/) (500 t, 42 MPa, cold-press specialist), HN500 (620 t, 52 MPa, maximum extraction)
- Batch size: 20–100 kg of crushed seed depending on barrel configuration
- Cycle: 30–40 minutes hot, about 2 hours cold
- Double-barrel versions nearly double throughput by loading one barrel while the other presses
- A bar-type slotted barrel typically leaves 6–8% residual oil in cake, versus 10–12% for old drilled barrels

### 7. Oil Filtration System

Crude oil leaving the press carries fine seed solids. Filtration turns it into a saleable, stable product.

- Pneumatic multi-layer filter press is the standard choice for hydraulic mills — no consumable cloth, fast cycles
- Primary: 100–200 mesh; fine: 200–300 mesh; polishing: 300–500 mesh for bottled premium oil
- Many small mills pair the filter with 24–48 hours of settling and skip the polishing stage

### 8. Oil Storage System

- 304 stainless steel tanks for cold-pressed and export oil; coated mild steel is acceptable for hot-pressed bulk oil
- Store cold-pressed oil below 25°C, away from light, under nitrogen if held more than 30 days
- Plan tank capacity for at least 3–5 days of production to decouple pressing from dispatch

### 9. Cake Handling and Refining (Optional)

- Cake drops onto a conveyor or into bins; hard cakes (copra, cottonseed) may need a cake breaker
- Cake is revenue, not waste: most seed cakes sell as protein-rich animal feed
- A refining line (degumming, neutralizing, bleaching, deodorizing) only makes sense above roughly 5 tonnes/day or for neutral bottled oil; below that, filtered crude and cold-pressed oil are the usual products

## Capacity Planning

### Capacity Requirements by Scale

| Scale | Daily Seed Input | Press Configuration | Typical Staff |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| Small workshop | 500–1,000 kg | 1 × HN325, single barrel | 2–3 |
| Medium mill | 1,000–2,500 kg | 1 × HN365/HN426, double barrel | 4–6 |
| Large mill | 2,500–5,000 kg | 2 presses or HN500 double barrel | 7–10 |

Remember that published hot-press tonnage assumes full 100 kg batches running back-to-back. Cold pressing takes three to four times longer per batch, so a line sold as "1 tonne per day hot" delivers roughly 300–700 kg cold depending on barrel configuration.

### Line Balance Rules

1. **Roasting** must outrun the press — target at least 2× the pressing rate so the press never waits.
2. **Crushing and conditioning** should match the pressing rate hour by hour.
3. **Filtration** must keep pace with oil output; a filter bottleneck backs crude oil into the press room.
4. **Storage** absorbs mismatches between production rhythm and truck collection: 3–5 days minimum.

A useful design trick is to size every station against the press's *peak* output (first 10 minutes of each cycle release the most oil), not its average.

## Layout Design

### Workflow Principles

1. **Straight-line or U-shaped flow** — seed enters one end, oil and cake leave the other; raw material never crosses finished product.
2. **Gravity where possible** — elevate cleaning and roasting so material drops into the crusher and press, saving conveyors.
3. **Maintenance access** — leave 1 metre around the press for barrel withdrawal and 2 metres in front for loading.
4. **Expansion room** — reserve floor space for a second barrel station or a second press from day one; moving an installed line is expensive.

### Space Requirements

| Scale | Production Area | Ceiling Height | Notes |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|-------|
| Small | 50–80 m² | 3 m | Cleaning at floor level |
| Medium | 100–150 m² | 3–4 m | Elevated cleaner, mezzanine possible |
| Large | 200–300 m² | 4–5 m | Overhead conveying, separate cake room |

Add roughly 30% for raw seed storage, packaging, and an office/lab corner.

## Utility Requirements

### Power

The press motor is 2.2 kW on all Haonuo models, but the line as a whole draws far more:

| Equipment | Typical Power |
|-----------|---------------|
| Hydraulic press | 2.2 kW (peak ~3 kW during pressure build-up) |
| Vibrating cleaner + destoner | 1.5–3 kW |
| Roaster (electric) | 15–40 kW; gas models reduce electrical load |
| Crusher | 3–7.5 kW |
| Filter press | 1.5–3 kW |
| Conveyors and pumps | 3–8 kW |

Budget **20–40 kVA** for a complete small or medium line, and confirm three-phase supply. See the [electrical requirements guide](/resources/oil-press-electrical-requirements-guide/) for generator sizing.

### Water and Fuel

- Washing, floor cleaning, and conditioner steam: 0.5–1 m³/day for a small mill
- Cooling water for hydraulic oil cooler in hot climates: 2–5 m³/day (recirculated)
- Gas or wood for roasting is often the largest operating energy cost after seed — price both locally before choosing an electric roaster

## Investment and Operating Cost Outline

A realistic budget covers more than the press. For a typical small-to-medium line the split looks roughly like this:

| Item | Share of Equipment Budget |
|------|---------------------------|
| Hydraulic press (single/double barrel) | 35–45% |
| Cleaning, dehulling, crushing | 15–20% |
| Roaster + conditioner | 15–25% |
| Filtration + tanks | 10–15% |
| Conveyors, installation, electrics | 10–15% |

On top of equipment, plan for sea or land freight, duties and local VAT, a 30–45 day commissioning period, and a spare-parts stock (seals, filter elements, O-rings). Running costs are dominated by seed — usually 80–90% of cost per litre — which is why yield and moisture discipline pay back faster than any machine discount.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Confirm target seed(s), daily tonnage, and hot versus cold product mix.
2. Lock the press model and barrel count; this fixes every other station's size.
3. List supporting equipment with capacities matched to peak press output.
4. Draft the floor plan and confirm ceiling height, power, and water.
5. Order with a spare-parts package and installation guidance; schedule operator training at commissioning.
6. Start with one seed, one recipe, and batch records before adding a second material.

## Conclusion

A complete oil production line is a chain whose strength is set by its slowest link, not its most expensive machine. Sizing cleaning, roasting, crushing, filtration, and storage around the press's real peak output — and budgeting for the full system rather than a single machine — is what separates profitable mills from crowded workshops.
