## Overview

Olive oil is the cornerstone of Mediterranean cuisine and one of the most valued cooking oils worldwide, with extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) commanding premium prices in global markets. A quality **olive oil press machine** is essential for olive growers and oil producers who want to extract the finest oil from their harvest while preserving the flavor, aroma, and nutritional properties that define premium olive oil.

Haonuo's olive oil pressing solution is built around our cold press hydraulic technology, which is the ideal method for producing extra virgin olive oil. Unlike traditional hammer mills and screw presses that can generate excessive heat and introduce metallic flavors, our hydraulic **olive oil press machine** applies gentle, uniform pressure at temperatures below 60°C, preserving the polyphenols, oleic acid, and delicate fruit flavors that distinguish truly exceptional olive oil.

This solution is designed for Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, and New World olive oil producers who demand the highest quality from their olive harvest.

**Key factors affecting yield:**
- **Olive variety**: Koroneiki (Greek), Picual (Spanish), and Frantoio (Italian) are among the highest-yielding varieties
- **Ripeness**: Fully ripe olives yield more oil but with milder flavor; early-harvest olives produce less oil but with more intense flavor and polyphenols
- **Harvest timing**: Olives should be pressed within 24 hours of harvest for best quality
- **Pressing temperature**: Cold pressing below 60°C preserves quality; temperatures above 27°C disqualify oil from "cold-pressed" designation

## Recommended Process

1. **Harvesting** — Pick olives at the optimal ripeness for your target oil style. Early harvest (green olives) produces robust, peppery oil. Late harvest (black olives) produces milder, buttery oil.
2. **Cleaning & Defoliating** — Remove leaves, twigs, and debris from harvested olives. Leaves impart bitter flavors to the oil. Wash olives to remove dust and pesticide residue.
3. **Cold Pressing** — Feed cleaned olives directly into the Cold Press Hydraulic Oil Press (HN426). The hydraulic press applies uniform pressure without generating excessive heat, preserving polyphenol content. Each batch processes in 30-40 minutes (hot press) or approximately 2 hours (cold press).
4. **Filtration** — Pass crude olive oil through the Pneumatic Oil Filter Press. Some premium producers prefer minimal filtration to retain more flavor, while others filter for clarity.
5. **Settling** — Allow filtered oil to settle for 2-4 weeks in stainless steel tanks. Natural settling lets remaining sediment and micro-particles deposit.
6. **Quality Testing** — Test for acidity (extra virgin must be below 0.8%), peroxide value, and sensory characteristics.
7. **Bottling** — Bottle olive oil in dark glass bottles or tin containers. Protect from light and heat to preserve quality.

## Recommended Equipment

**How much does an olive oil press machine cost?**

**How does hydraulic pressing compare to traditional olive oil extraction?**
Traditional olive oil extraction uses hammer mills to crush olives into paste, then centrifuges to separate oil. Our hydraulic press is simpler, more affordable, and ideal for small to medium producers. It produces genuine cold-pressed extra virgin olive oil without the complexity and cost of centrifuge systems.

**What makes extra virgin olive oil different from regular olive oil?**
Extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) is the highest grade, produced by cold pressing without any chemical processing or refining. It must have acidity below 0.8% and pass sensory evaluation for fruitiness, bitterness, and pungency. Our hydraulic cold press is specifically designed to produce oil that meets EVOO standards.

**How quickly should olives be pressed after harvest?**
Ideally within 24 hours. Olives begin to oxidize immediately after harvest, and delays beyond 48 hours significantly degrade oil quality. If immediate pressing is not possible, store olives in ventilated containers at cool temperatures.

**Can I press olives from different varieties together?**
Yes, blending varieties is common and can produce excellent oil. Many premium olive oils are blends of complementary varieties. However, if you want to produce single-variety oils for the premium market, press each variety separately.
