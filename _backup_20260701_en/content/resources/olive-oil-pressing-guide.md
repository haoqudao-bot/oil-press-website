Olive oil is often called "liquid gold" — and for good reason. It is the cornerstone of Mediterranean cuisine, a staple in Middle Eastern cooking, and one of the most health-promoting foods on the planet. The global olive oil market exceeds $14 billion and continues to grow as consumers worldwide discover the benefits of this remarkable oil.

For producers in the Middle East, North Africa, and the Mediterranean, **olive oil pressing** represents both a cultural tradition and a significant business opportunity. This guide covers everything you need to know about producing premium **extra virgin olive oil** — from understanding oil grades to selecting the right **olive oil press machine**, controlling temperature during pressing, and maximizing your yield and quality.

## The Olive Oil Industry: A Middle East and Mediterranean Focus

The olive oil industry is deeply rooted in the Mediterranean and Middle East, where olive trees have been cultivated for over 6,000 years. Today, the region remains the epicenter of global production.

**Spain** is the world's largest producer, generating approximately 1.4 million metric tons annually. **Italy** and **Greece** follow as the second and third largest producers. However, the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region is increasingly significant:

- **Tunisia** — The largest olive oil producer in Africa and the 4th largest globally, with over 300,000 metric tons annually. Olive cultivation covers 1.8 million hectares — one-third of the country's arable land.
- **Turkey** — Produces approximately 200,000 metric tons annually, with the Aegean region being the primary production area.
- **Morocco** — Rapidly expanding production, currently around 150,000 metric tons per year.
- **Syria and Palestine** — Traditional producers with centuries of olive oil heritage, though production has been affected by conflict.
- **Saudi Arabia and Jordan** — Growing olive oil industries with government support for agricultural diversification.

The MENA region produces over 800,000 metric tons of olive oil annually, and demand continues to grow both domestically and for export to premium markets in Europe, North America, and Asia.

## Understanding Olive Oil Grades

Not all olive oil is created equal. The International Olive Council (IOC) defines strict standards for olive oil grades, and understanding these is essential for any producer.

### Extra Virgin Olive Oil (EVOO)

The highest grade of olive oil, commanding premium prices worldwide.

| Parameter | IOC Standard |
|-----------|-------------|
| Free acidity | ≤ 0.8% (as oleic acid) |
| Peroxide value | ≤ 20 meq O₂/kg |
| Absorbency (K232) | ≤ 2.50 |
| Absorbency (K270) | ≤ 0.22 |
| Sensory defects | 0 (median) |
| Fruitness | > 0 (median) |
| Production method | Cold pressing only, no refining |

**Extra virgin olive oil** must be produced entirely by mechanical means at temperatures below 27°C (80.6°F). No chemical treatment or refining is permitted. This is where cold press technology becomes essential — any overheating during pressing will increase acidity and create sensory defects, disqualifying the oil from EVOO status.

### Virgin Olive Oil

| Parameter | IOC Standard |
|-----------|-------------|
| Free acidity | ≤ 2.0% |
| Sensory defects | ≤ 3.5 (median) |
| Production method | Mechanical pressing only |

A lower grade than EVOO, virgin olive oil has higher acidity and may have minor sensory defects. It is still produced by mechanical means only and is suitable for cooking.

### Refined Olive Oil

Olive oil that has been refined to remove defects, acidity, and off-flavors. It is typically blended with virgin olive oil to create what is sold as "olive oil" or "pure olive oil" in supermarkets. This grade has minimal flavor and is used primarily for cooking at high temperatures.

### Olive Pomace Oil

Extracted from the olive pomace (the solid residue after pressing) using chemical solvents. This is the lowest grade and cannot be labeled as "olive oil" in most jurisdictions — it must be labeled as "olive pomace oil."

**For maximum profitability, your goal should be to produce extra virgin olive oil.** EVOO commands prices 3-5 times higher than refined olive oil, and the global demand for premium EVOO continues to grow. The key to EVOO production is **cold pressing** with strict temperature control.

## The Cold Pressing Process

Cold pressing is the only method permitted for producing extra virgin olive oil. Here is the step-by-step process:

### 1. Harvesting

The quality of your olive oil begins in the grove. Harvest timing is critical:

- **Early harvest** (green olives) — Produces robust, peppery oil with higher polyphenol content and longer shelf life. Lower yield (15-20% oil by weight) but premium pricing.
- **Mid-season harvest** (turning color) — Balanced flavor profile with moderate polyphenol content. Yield of 18-25%.
- **Late harvest** (fully ripe, black olives) — Milder, buttery oil with lower polyphenol content. Higher yield (22-28%) but shorter shelf life and lower market price.

Olives should be processed within 24 hours of harvesting — ideally within 4-6 hours. Delayed processing causes enzymatic degradation that increases free acidity and creates sensory defects.

### 2. Cleaning and Washing

Remove leaves, twigs, and debris. Wash olives with clean water to remove dust and soil. Leaves in particular can impart bitter flavors and increase chlorophyll content, which accelerates oxidation.

### 3. Crushing (Milling)

Olives are crushed into a paste using a hammer mill or stone mill. The paste consists of oil, water, and solid material. Modern hammer mills are preferred for their speed and consistency, though some premium producers still use traditional stone mills for their gentler crushing action.

### 4. Malaxation (Mixing)

The olive paste is slowly mixed (malaxed) at 25-27°C for 30-45 minutes. This is the most critical step for oil quality:

- **Temperature must stay below 27°C** to maintain EVOO classification. This is where your **olive oil press machine** must have precise temperature control.
- Mixing allows small oil droplets to coalesce into larger ones, improving extraction efficiency.
- Excessive mixing time or temperature increases oxidation and reduces polyphenol content.

### 5. Pressing / Centrifugation

The malaxed paste is pressed to separate the oil from the solids and water:

- **Traditional hydraulic press** — Uses hydraulic pressure to squeeze the paste in mats. Produces high-quality oil but is labor-intensive and lower capacity.
- **Modern centrifuge (decanter)** — Uses centrifugal force to separate oil, water, and solids. Higher capacity and more consistent, but requires more energy.

### 6. Separation and Filtration

The pressed oil still contains water and fine particles. A vertical centrifuge or settling tank separates the oil from the vegetable water. Optional filtration through a filter press produces crystal-clear oil, though some premium producers prefer unfiltered oil for its fuller flavor.

**Key factors affecting yield:**
- **Olive variety** — Some varieties like Picual have higher oil content than Arbequina
- **Irrigation** — Adequately irrigated olives have higher oil content
- **Processing speed** — Processing within 4-6 hours of harvest maximizes yield and quality
- **Malaxation temperature** — Optimal at 25-27°C; higher temperatures increase yield but risk EVOO classification
- **Press pressure** — Higher pressure extracts more oil but may increase impurities

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What temperature should olive oil be pressed at?**
For extra virgin olive oil classification, the entire pressing process must stay below 27°C (80.6°F). This includes malaxation, pressing, and any other processing steps. Haonuo's cold press hydraulic oil press includes temperature monitoring and control to ensure compliance with EVOO standards.

**How many olives do I need to produce one liter of olive oil?**
On average, you need 5-10 kg of olives to produce one liter of olive oil, depending on the olive variety, ripeness, and pressing efficiency. Early-harvest green olives require more weight per liter but produce premium EVOO with higher polyphenol content.

**Can I use the same machine for olives and other oilseeds?**
Yes, Haonuo's hydraulic oil press can process olives, sesame, peanut, walnut, and other oilseeds. However, for olive oil production, you need additional equipment for crushing the olives into paste and malaxation (mixing) before pressing. The hydraulic press handles the final pressing step.

**What is the shelf life of extra virgin olive oil?**
Properly produced and stored EVOO has a shelf life of 18-24 months from the pressing date. Store in dark glass bottles or stainless steel containers at 14-18°C, away from light and heat. Early-harvest EVOO with high polyphenol content has the longest shelf life due to the antioxidant properties of polyphenols.

**How can I test if my olive oil meets EVOO standards?**
You need to test for free acidity (must be ≤ 0.8%), peroxide value (must be ≤ 20 meq/kg), and sensory evaluation by a trained tasting panel. Many countries have local laboratories that perform these tests. The key production factors are harvesting at the right time, processing within 24 hours, and maintaining pressing temperature below 27°C.
